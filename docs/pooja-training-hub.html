Module 01

Start Here

Read this once, in full, before anything else. Everything downstream assumes you know this.

What is CareerRai

CareerRai is a CAT preparation platform built around execution, not more content. The core problem it solves is the gap between "I know what I should study" and "I actually studied the right thing today."

The product loop: Plan → Study → Log → Plan adjusts. CareerRai uses the student's actual preparation data — not what they intended to do — to keep handing them the next right thing to study.

The mission (top priority document in the company)

"How do we build a free, massively used student platform that continuously learns how Indian students actually study, struggle, decide, improve, interact, and eventually achieve outcomes — and use small amounts of monetisation only to keep that machine running?"

Practically: the free product comes first. CareerRai is compared internally to Duolingo and Reddit — not coaching institutes. Revenue keeps the lights on; it is never the goal. A tactic that makes the free experience worse to convert someone is against company policy, not just bad practice.

What CareerRai is NOT

Never casually describe CareerRai as: a traditional coaching institute · a lecture platform · a replacement for all study material · a generic motivation app · a random mentor marketplace where students browse and compare mentors by price · a platform that guarantees percentile improvement · a platform that guarantees IIM admission · "better than coaching" as an unsupported claim.

Correct framing: coaching provides teaching/content. CareerRai provides execution, tracking, accountability, and personalised guidance from someone who has actually seen the student's real prep data.

Your role

You're the human bridge between a student's doubt and their decision to trust CareerRai with their preparation. A CAT aspirant is handing us a year of their life and, often, their parents' money — every conversation either earns that or spends it.

Product Knowledge

Every feature answers "why does this matter to the student?" — never a feature dump.

The core product loop

Student enters CareerRai, gives real info about their prep
↓
System understands them: prep level, available time, topic coverage, weak areas, past attempts, study behaviour
↓
Student gets a clear "what to do today"
↓
Student studies
↓
Student logs what actually happened — including "I studied nothing today." The log is information, not punishment.
↓
Tomorrow's plan adapts to what actually happened.

The two products

FREE

Study plan

/student/today, /student/plan — a personalised, adapting daily plan from the student's own prep data. This isn't a lite version of anything — it's the actual mission.

PAID

Buddy

/student/buddy — a real, verified IIM student/alumnus, matched to the student. Live weekly sessions + daily chat guidance.

The distinction that matters most in every conversation: don't sell the IIM tag alone. Not "IIM student hai" — but "this person understands YOUR preparation and can give specific feedback on it."

Mentors are reviewed, not self-serve

Every mentor profile goes through review and a background check before it's ever shown to a student. If asked how mentors are verified: "every mentor is manually reviewed and background-checked before going live, and their profile shows verifiable proof — LinkedIn, CAT percentile, college — you can check yourself." Exact background-check steps aren't documented for you to recite — escalate if pushed further.

7 · The refund threshold is stated three different ways NEW — 13 AUG

Flagged loudest because getting it wrong denies a real student a refund they actually qualify for.

SourceWhat it says
Founder, 13 Aug 2026"21 day login after subscription"
Public /refunds page (live, student-readable)20 logged study days within the first month
Live code (request-refund/route.ts)REQUIRED_DAYS = 20, counting daily_reports within 30 days of profiles.created_at

Three separate discrepancies, not one:

  • 21 vs 20. Quote 21 and a student sitting on exactly 20 logged days is told they don't qualify — while our own public page tells them they do.
  • "Login" vs "logged study day." Not the same action. The code counts a student recording what they studied. Opening the app 21 times without logging once would pass a "login" rule and fail the current one. This changes who qualifies, not just how many.
  • "After subscription" vs from signup. The founder's phrasing measures from the subscription date; the code measures from account creation. For anyone who used the free product before paying, these are wildly different windows.
Why #3 is more than wording — it's a live defect today. A student who signs up free in June, subscribes in August, then logs 25 study days as a paying member is currently refused — their 30-day window closed in July, before they ever paid. Conversely someone who logged 20 days as a free user and subscribed months later qualifies instantly, having never experienced the paid product. The founder's own phrasing ("after subscription") is the version that matches what the guarantee is for.
Decision needed, three parts: (a) 20 or 21? (b) logged study days or logins? (c) does the window start at subscription or signup? Whichever way (a) and (b) go, (c) looks like a bug worth fixing in code regardless — but it changes who gets money back, so it's a founder call, not one to make quietly. Until all three are answered, Pooja quotes the public page's 20-logged-days version.

4 · Refund policy specificity informational, not a contradiction

The founder's draft describes the policy only as "first-month value-based," which reads as subjective. The live policy is a specific, objective 20-day threshold. No decision needed — Module 06 already uses the precise version — flagged so the founder knows his own phrasing understates how firm the rule actually is.

Small pieces of Design v2's real-lead psychology (the "3 fears" framework, follow-up patterns) are already folded into Module 04 and Module 08 here, but Design v2 hasn't been reproduced in full.

Decision needed: replace, run alongside as a separate practice track, or fully absorb Design v2's archetype table into this hub? Recommendation (not a decision): absorb — it's the only source built from real lead data.
